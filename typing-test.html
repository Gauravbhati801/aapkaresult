Frequently Asked Questions

Typing Test FAQs & Speed Guidelines

Q1. What is WPM and CPM, and how are they calculated?

WPM stands for Words Per Minute, and CPM stands for Characters Per Minute. In standard typing metrics, 1 word is counted as 5 keystrokes (including letters, symbols, and spaces). Your Net WPM is computed by taking your total correct characters typed in 60 seconds and dividing by 5.

Q2. What typing speed is required for SSC, Railway & Police exams?

For SSC CHSL / CGL (LDC, DEO, Tax Assistant): 35 WPM in English or 30 WPM in Hindi (with 93–95% accuracy). For Railway RRB NTPC: 30 WPM in English or 25 WPM in Hindi. For High Court & Secretariat clerical posts: 35 to 45+ WPM is required.

Q3. What is the difference between Gross Speed and Net Speed?

Gross Speed is the raw count of all words typed per minute, including mistakes. Net Speed is your actual speed after deducting penalties for errors. Examination boards like SSC and RRB qualify candidates solely based on Net WPM.

Q4. How can I increase my typing speed and finger muscle memory?

Master the standard 10-finger touch typing method starting from the Home Row (ASDF for left hand, JKL; for right hand). Use the tactile ridges on the F and J keys to align your index fingers without looking down. Focus on 95%+ accuracy first; speed naturally follows.

Q5. Is Backspace allowed during government typing speed tests?

In most government examinations (such as SSC, Railway, and High Court tests), the Backspace key is allowed within the currently typed word. However, frequent backspacing drastically slows your Net WPM. Practice steady rhythm with minimal corrections to achieve top scores.

Q6. What is considered a good typing speed for candidates?

An everyday user types between 38–40 WPM. For competitive recruitment examinations, a speed of 40–50 WPM with 95%+ accuracy is considered a safe qualifying score. Professional typists, stenographers, and data operators comfortably type 65 to 80+ WPM.
