UPSC IAS Civil Services (CSE) Complete Exam Blueprint & Syllabus 2026

Board / Commission: Union Public Service Commission (UPSC), New Delhi Mode: Official Portal Guide Status: Live & Verified 2026

UPSC IAS Civil Services (CSE) Complete Exam Blueprint & Syllabus 2026
IAS, IPS, IFS & Central Group-A Services: Prelims, Mains (1750 Marks) & Personality Test (275 Marks)
Exam Authority: Union Public Service Commission (UPSC), New Delhi 🏛️ UPSC CSE COMPLETE BLUEPRINT & SYLLABUS

Short Information : The Union Public Service Commission (UPSC) conducts the Civil Services Examination (CSE) every year in three stages: Preliminary Examination (Objective), Main Examination (Written Descriptive), and Personality Test (Interview) to recruit officers for IAS, IPS, IFS, IRS, and allied Group-A/B Central Services. Review the complete paper-wise marks distribution, CSAT qualifying rules, and GS Mains syllabus topics below.

Stage 1: Prelims Pattern (400 Marks)
  • Paper-I (General Studies) : 100 Questions | 200 Marks (2 Hours)
  • Paper-II (CSAT Aptitude) : 80 Questions | 200 Marks (2 Hours)
  • CSAT Qualifying Cutoff : Minimum 33% (66 Marks Mandatory)
  • Negative Marking : 1/3rd Marks Deducted per Wrong Answer
  • Prelims Merit Basis : Decided Strictly by GS Paper-I Marks
Stage 2 & 3: Mains (1750) + Interview (275)
  • Qualifying Papers (A & B) : Indian Language + English (300 Marks Each, 25% Pass)
  • Essay Paper (Paper-I) : 250 Marks (3 Hours)
  • GS Papers I, II, III & IV : 4 Papers × 250 Marks = 1,000 Marks
  • Optional Subject (Paper I & II) : 2 Papers × 250 Marks = 500 Marks
  • Personality Test (Interview) : 275 Marks (Total Final Merit = 2,025 Marks)
GS Paper-Wise Core Syllabus Breakdown
  1. Prelims GS & CSAT: Current Events, Indian History & Freedom Struggle, Indian & World Geography, Indian Polity & Constitution, Economy, Environment & Ecology, General Science, Comprehension & Logical Reasoning.
  2. Mains GS-I: Indian Heritage & Culture, Modern Indian & World History, Post-Independence Consolidation, Indian Society, and Physical/Human Geography.
  3. Mains GS-II: Indian Constitution, Parliament, Federalism, Social Justice, Welfare Schemes, Governance, Transparency, and International Relations.
  4. Mains GS-III: Indian Economic Development, Agriculture, Science & Technology, Environment & Disaster Management, and Internal Security.
  5. Mains GS-IV (Ethics): Ethics & Human Interface, Attitude, Emotional Intelligence, Moral Thinkers, Public/Civil Service Values, Probity in Governance, and Case Studies.

Q2. Is there negative marking in this examination?

Yes, unless explicitly stated otherwise (e.g., NTA UGC NET has zero negative marking), most objective exams deduct 1/3rd or 1/4th marks for incorrect answers. Check the Marking Scheme section above for exact rules.

AapkaResult.in Editorial Analysis: UPSC CSE Prelims CSAT Elimination Trend, GS Mains Weightage & Optional Subject Strategy

1. Why Paper-II (CSAT 33% Qualifying Rule) Has Become the Biggest Prelims Filter: In recent UPSC Civil Services Preliminary cycles, while the General Studies Paper-I cutoff for the Unreserved category has settled around 75 to 88 marks out of 200 (due to multi-statement elimination-proof pair questions), thousands of high-scoring GS candidates fail to clear Prelims because they fall short of the mandatory 66.67 marks (33%) in GS Paper-II (CSAT). Aspirants must dedicate at least 3 hours weekly from January to May specifically to Number System, Permutations, Data Sufficiency, and Logical Inference Reading Comprehension passages.

2. High-ROI Subjects in the 1,750-Mark Written Mains Examination: Although aspirants spend 70% of their preparation time on Static GS (History, Geography, Polity, Economy), final service allocation into IAS, IPS, or IFS is overwhelmingly decided by three high-scoring pillars totaling 1,000 out of 1,750 written marks: the Essay Paper (250 marks), GS Paper-IV Ethics, Integrity & Aptitude (250 marks), and the Optional Subject Papers I & II (500 marks).

3. Standard Booklist vs. Current Affairs Integration: Instead of accumulating dozens of coaching notes, top rankers integrate foundational NCERTs (Classes 6–12) and standard texts (Laxmikanth for Polity, Spectrum for Modern India, GC Leong for Geography) with PIB notifications, Union Budget/Economic Survey chapters, and previous 10 years' UPSC PYQ thematic analysis.
